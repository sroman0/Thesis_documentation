Chapter 7 Agentic Writing Workflow

This page contains the complete agent pipeline and the prompts needed to produce the final chapter, Conclusions and Future Work. The target is a concise chapter of approximately three to four thesis pages. All agent-to-user artefacts are valid standalone HTML.

Editorial Decision

Chapter 7 closes the argument; it does not retell the thesis. It should state what was built, what the controlled evaluation supports, where the resulting claims stop and which next steps follow directly from those limits.

The recommended body length is roughly 1,400-1,800 words, subject to the actual thesis layout. The chapter should contain no source listings, figures, large tables, YAML examples, command sequences or new experimental results. Four short sections are sufficient.

Do not write the abstract in this workflow. The abstract remains a separate final task after Chapter 7 has been approved. Do not introduce an official product name: use the proposed tool, the prototype, the monitoring agent or the proposed system.

Chapter Boundaries

MaterialPrimary ownerChapter 7 treatment
eBPF theory and related toolsChapter 2Do not explain them again.
Requirements and architectureChapter 3Recall only the design objective needed to frame the outcome.
Kernel-space implementationChapter 4Summarise the implemented contribution in one compact passage.
Go runtime, policy and detection implementationChapter 5State the achieved end-to-end capability without package-level detail.
Experimental protocol and numerical evidenceChapter 6Use only its final, qualified findings; do not repeat methods or full tables.
Overall answer, broader limitations and prioritised future workChapter 7Primary material.

The conclusions must preserve the distinction between observation, detection and enforcement. They must not claim cluster-wide correlation, general anomaly discovery, complete ATT&CK coverage, loss-free transport, total-system overhead, general detection accuracy or production readiness.

Provisional Structure

  1. 7.1 Summary of the Work. One narrative synthesis of the problem, the kernel/user-space solution and the implemented monitoring pipeline.
  2. 7.2 Main Results. The strongest supported conclusions from the cross-Pod functional campaign and the scoped monitor-cgroup performance comparison.
  3. 7.3 Limitations. A compact synthesis of technical and experimental boundaries, without reproducing the threats-to-validity taxonomy from Chapter 6.
  4. 7.4 Future Work. A prioritised sequence tied directly to the limitations, followed by a brief closing paragraph.

The synthesis agent may merge the first two sections if the final length becomes unbalanced, but should not create a deep subsection hierarchy. The final paragraph must close the thesis rather than merely announce future engineering tasks.

Acceptance Gates

  • Gate A: all three audit reports exist and agree on the supported result boundary.
  • Gate B: the editorial synthesis contains no stale future-work item and is explicitly approved.
  • Gate C: Chapter 7 stays within the agreed scale and compiles without new unresolved references.
  • Gate D: the fresh reviewer finds no unsupported conclusion or high-severity inconsistency.
  • Gate E: the final human reading confirms that the chapter closes the thesis naturally and does not sound like an executive summary assembled from earlier paragraphs.
Expected outcome. A short final chapter that answers the objective stated in Chapter 1, preserves the experimental qualifications established in Chapter 6 and leaves the reader with a credible, prioritised path beyond the prototype.
