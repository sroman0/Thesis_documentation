Chapter 7 Editorial Closure and Redundancy Audit

Scope: Chapters 1-6, the definitive outline, editorial rules, bibliography and glossary. This audit treats the thesis as one cumulative argument and does not verify implementation internals.

Editorial Verdict

Chapter 7 should close the argument, not replay it. Its new contribution is an overall answer to the thesis objective, a hierarchy of what the evidence supports, and a prioritised path from current limitations to future work. Background, architecture, implementation mechanics, experimental procedure and the threats-to-validity taxonomy already have clear owners.

The strongest structure is three sections rather than the four canonical sections currently listed. Summary of the Work and Main Results should be merged into one evaluative synthesis. Keeping them separate would encourage a second version of Chapter 1's objective followed immediately by a second version of Chapter 6's evaluation summary.

Material Not to Repeat

Chapter 7 should avoid both verbatim reuse and lightly paraphrased versions of the following explanations:

  • “The objective of this thesis is to design, implement and evaluate...” followed by the full Chapter 1 formulation.
  • The statement that applications may omit activity which remains visible at the kernel boundary.
  • A definition of eBPF, verifier acceptance, JIT compilation, maps, BTF or CO-RE.
  • The complete kernel-space versus user-space responsibility split.
  • The definitions of event, policy, detector and alert, or the distinction between logical events and physical hooks.
  • The explanation that requests, security mediation, committed transitions and return outcomes expose different stages.
  • The 136-byte context, indexed argument stream, 32,000-byte workspace or perf-buffer mechanics.
  • Lists of hook families or captured fields.
  • Loader, registry, decoder, YAML, correlation-plan, state-pruning, deduplication or Zap details.
  • The Cross-Pod deployment procedure, AES-beta exploit sequence and four raw evidence records.
  • The full performance methodology, P95 definition, cgroup-v1 accounting and per-run measurements.
  • The exact Chapter 6 sentence “The experiment met its three scoped objectives” or a second three-objective enumeration.
  • The internal/construct/external/conclusion-validity taxonomy.
  • The already established disclaimers as a dense negative list: no enforcement, no cross-node correlation, no complete coverage, no total-overhead result and no production readiness.
Compression rule: a limitation may reappear only when it performs new work by motivating a specific priority. If it merely restates Chapter 6, remove it.

  6. Performance conclusion. Use one paragraph for the filtered mean of 0.46% of one core, the 5% project gate and the approximately 92% filtered/unfiltered difference. Keep “monitor-container cgroup CPU” attached to every quantitative conclusion.
  7. Scope boundary. Group technical limits: node-local deterministic detection, no enforcement, target-kernel compatibility and UID admission that is not workload identity.
  8. Evidence boundary. Group evaluation limits: one scenario, one node, three runs per profile, high utilisation and incomplete eBPF cost attribution. Do not reproduce four validity categories.
  9. Priority one. Strengthen evidence before feature breadth: repeat on quieter and varied nodes, improve kernel-cost attribution, add sustained-load and broader benign/offensive scenarios.
  10. Priority two. Harden deployment and identity: reduce privilege where feasible and replace narrow numeric UID assumptions with reliable workload-aware selection supplied by a wider platform.
  11. Priority three. Extend detection only after the first two priorities: additional event families, carefully bounded correlations and optional central aggregation for context unavailable to a node-local agent.
  12. Closing paragraph. Reconnect to the initial problem of turning heterogeneous kernel observations into useful security evidence under real compatibility and resource constraints.
Final-line direction: end with the durable lesson of the work: useful runtime monitoring depends not on collecting the largest event catalogue, but on preserving trustworthy evidence through explicit contracts and bounded interpretation. Do not end with a list of pending engineering tasks.

Promotional and Novelty Audit

The current thesis is generally restrained. Chapter 2 explicitly states that the mechanisms are not claimed as novel in isolation and positions the work as a compact integration tailored to the selected enterprise kernel. Chapter 7 must preserve that boundary.

  • Do not use novel, innovative, unique, state of the art, first, comprehensive, robust, scalable or production-ready.
  • Do not claim complete event coverage, ATT&CK coverage, detection accuracy, portability, loss-free transport or low total overhead.
  • Do not claim that the experiment proves causality. Prefer supports, shows under the evaluated conditions, was associated with and is consistent with.
  • Use implemented contribution for the event framework, event contract, Go runtime and bounded detector pipeline. Do not convert integration work into research novelty without a separate comparative argument.
  • “Demonstrates” is acceptable only for a directly observed capability with its boundary in the same sentence; supports is safer in the final synthesis.

  • The opening answers the thesis objective rather than announcing a summary.
  • The chapter is 1,400-1,800 words and approximately 3-4 rendered pages.
  • It contains no chapter-by-chapter recap.
  • It contains no figures, tables, listings, YAML, commands or experimental protocol.
  • Every implemented contribution is described by its consequence, not its source-level mechanism.
  • The functional result is node-local and Cross-Pod, not cross-node or cluster-wide.
  • The collective detector result remains scenario-specific and deterministic.
  • The 0.46%, 5% and approximately 92% values retain the monitor-container cgroup boundary.
  • No absence of a loss diagnostic is presented as proof of loss-free transport.
  • No client outcome or three benign controls are used to infer general detection accuracy.
  • Limitations are synthesised, not copied from the four Chapter 6 validity categories.
  • Each future-work priority resolves a stated limitation.
  • Future work is ordered by evidence quality and operational safety before feature breadth.
  • Current capabilities and future capabilities are grammatically and conceptually distinct.
  • No unsupported novelty, superiority, production-readiness or enforcement claim appears.
  • Terminology follows the established thesis usage and British English.
  • No new acronym or citation is introduced without need.
  • The final paragraph closes the original problem rather than ending on a task list.
