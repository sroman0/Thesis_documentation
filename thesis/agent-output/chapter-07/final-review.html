2. Findings Ordered by Severity

Major — F1. The final general lesson is not established by the thesis evidence

Location: Chapter 7, lines 172–175.

Text at issue: the thesis says useful runtime monitoring “depends less on collecting the largest possible event catalogue than on preserving selective, well-defined evidence”.

Why this matters: Chapters 1–5 motivate selective evidence, and Chapter 6 evaluates one four-event detector under filtered and unfiltered admission. No experiment or related-work comparison tests event-catalogue breadth against evidence quality. The comparative “depends less on … than on …” construction therefore converts a design preference and a single-scenario result into a general conclusion.

Required edit: restrict the sentence to the demonstrated result. For example: “For the evaluated node-local scenario, selective, well-defined evidence was sufficient to carry the configured observation sequence from kernel hooks to an alert.” The final paragraph can then retain its useful closure without asserting an unevaluated general law.

Major — F2. The available rendering violates the 3–4 page budget

Location: whole chapter.

Evidence: the source contains 1,465 whitespace-delimited tokens. A fresh isolated 11pt report build using the thesis paragraph spacing reached five pages, matching the writing report. The first pass parsed the chapter and also reported one 6.18 pt overfull box in the paragraph at lines 24–35. A complete authoritative build could not reach chapter processing because the installed TeX distribution lacks biblatex.sty.

Required edit: reduce the chapter enough to render within four pages in the authoritative thesis layout. The best cuts are implementation mechanics in Section 7.1, the optional operational-diagnostic paragraph in Section 7.2, repeated validity language in Section 7.3 and detail within the five roadmap paragraphs. Rebuild before choosing an exact word target; rendered length is the binding constraint.

Moderate — F3. An immediate registry defect is mixed into the primary future-work roadmap

Locations: lines 90–92 and 122–129.

Evidence: make test-registry fails at TestPublicProbesHaveDecoderSpecs because security_socket_sendmsg is registered as public without a decoder schema. Source inspection shows that it is a support probe implied by cgroup network events and lacks the internal: true marker used by adjacent support probes.

Assessment: Chapter 7 accurately discloses the problem, so this is not a hidden capability overclaim. However, correcting a known one-line classification defect is a release/acceptance gate, not the first research or engineering direction beyond the thesis. Its inclusion under “The first priority” blurs defect correction with the genuinely forward-looking single-source contract work. It also weakens the unqualified opening statement that the objective was achieved, because NFR-03 requires a consistent kernel-to-user-space contract.

Required edit: either fix and verify the defect before submission and remove the snapshot-specific sentence, or retain it explicitly as a current acceptance caveat. Keep generation of shared contract metadata and mandatory consistency checks as the future direction.

Moderate — F4. Existing decoder robustness tests are presented too broadly as future work

Location: lines 141–147, especially “Canonical decoder fixtures should exercise malformed and boundary-sized records.”

Evidence: current tests already cover records whose arguments exceed 256 bytes, truncated string-array elements, invalid UTF-8, and truncated credential payloads. The decoder suite is incomplete—for example, the future case for cross-language canonical fixtures and full record-boundary invariants remains valid—but malformed-record testing is not wholly absent.

Required edit: say that existing decoder tests should be extended with C/Go canonical fixtures and complete boundary/truncation coverage. This preserves the real gap without presenting implemented tests as future work.

Moderate — F5. The Rocky Linux compatibility statement has a wider grammatical subject than the evaluated path

Location: lines 24–34.

Issue: “these mechanisms” can be read as the whole implemented event and attachment surface operating on the verified kernel. Chapter 6 validates the selected cross-Pod producer set and detector path, not every hook family, attachment route or logical event. Chapter 4 documents compatibility adaptations but does not experimentally establish complete runtime coverage.

Required edit: replace the broad result with “supports the evaluated mechanisms on Rocky Linux 8.10” or separate implementation intent from evaluated operation. Use the canonical environment name Rocky Linux 8.10.

Minor — F6. Sections 7.1–7.3 repeat more mechanics and validity prose than the page budget permits

Section 7.1 repeats the Chapter 3 division of responsibilities and several Chapter 4/5 summary details: complementary stages, direct and paired producers, support-program planning, CO-RE branches, schema-directed decoding and detector forms. Section 7.2 closely restates the Chapter 6 evaluation summary, while the last paragraph of Section 7.3 condenses most of Chapter 6’s external, construct and conclusion-validity qualifications. A conclusion must synthesise these points, but their present density contributes directly to the five-page result. Preserve the objective answer, the two scoped results and the few limitations that motivate the roadmap; remove mechanism-level enumeration.

Minor — F7. Priority 5 combines two limitations with different status

Reduced deployment privilege follows directly from the privileged, unconfined laboratory deployment and is a clear primary engineering priority. A versioned export contract for optional aggregation follows from an intentional node-local scope boundary rather than a demonstrated defect. Keeping both in one “fifth priority” makes the integration extension appear equally necessary. Retain least privilege as the priority and mark export/aggregation as an optional follow-on, consistent with Chapter 3.

Minor — F8. The opening should acknowledge the unresolved assurance qualification

Lines 4–11 answer the Chapter 1 objective clearly, and Chapters 3–6 support the design, implementation and scoped evaluation. Nevertheless, “achieved its scoped objective” reads categorically before the later registry inconsistency is disclosed. If the defect remains at submission, use “substantially achieved its scoped objective” or append a compact assurance qualification. If the defect is fixed and the registry suite passes, the current wording is acceptable.
